## Project 01: Stoichiometry of Solutions — Determination of Acetic Acid Content in Vinegar

**Subject:** General Chemistry Laboratory (CM011L)  
**Type:** Group Laboratory Experiment and Technical Report  
**Date:** September 2026

### Project Overview

This laboratory experiment focused on determining the concentration of acetic acid in commercial vinegar using acid-base titration and stoichiometric calculations.

My group and I analyzed vinegar samples by reacting them with a standardized sodium hydroxide solution. We used phenolphthalein as an indicator to identify the endpoint of the reaction and performed mathematical calculations to determine the percentage of acetic acid present in the sample.

### Objectives

The main objective was to determine the percentage by weight of acetic acid in vinegar while applying the principles of solution stoichiometry, neutralization reactions, and volumetric analysis.

### Results and Findings

The experiment involved three titration trials using 0.2129 M sodium hydroxide.

The calculated acetic acid concentrations were 3.56%, 3.79%, and 3.92% by weight, giving an **average concentration of 3.76% (w/w)**.

The differences between the trials demonstrated the importance of precise measurements, careful endpoint determination, and proper laboratory techniques.

### What I Learned

Through this experiment, I gained a better understanding of how mathematical calculations can be used to determine the composition of a substance.

I also learned that accurate results depend not only on applying the correct formulas but also on collecting reliable experimental data. This is relevant to AI Engineering because data quality, precision, and careful analysis are equally important when working with computational systems and mathematical models.

## Project 02: Dissolved Oxygen Content of Water

**Subject:** General Chemistry Laboratory (CM011L)  
**Type:** Group Laboratory Experiment and Technical Report  
**Date:** September 2026

### Project Overview

This laboratory activity focused on determining the dissolved oxygen concentration of a water sample using the Winkler iodometric method.

Dissolved oxygen is an important indicator of water quality because it helps assess the conditions available to support aquatic organisms.

My group and I analyzed a water sample collected from a laboratory sink at Mapúa University. Through chemical reactions, titration, and stoichiometric calculations, we determined the amount of oxygen dissolved in the sample.

### Objectives

The main objective was to measure the dissolved oxygen concentration of the water sample and interpret the results using the water-quality classifications provided in the laboratory worksheet.

### Results and Findings

Both experimental trials produced a dissolved oxygen concentration of **5.00 mg/L**.

Based on the interpretation guidelines provided, this value falls within the range classified as **Fair general water quality**.

The identical results from the two trials indicated good repeatability under the experimental conditions.

### What I Learned

This activity helped me understand how numerical measurements can be used to evaluate environmental conditions.

I learned that data must be interpreted carefully because factors such as temperature, sample handling, and experimental errors can influence the results.

As an AI Engineering student, I find this relevant because modern technologies can use environmental data for monitoring, analysis, and prediction. The experiment showed me how reliable measurements can provide a foundation for data-driven applications.

## Project 03: Calorimetry — Determination of Calorimeter Heat Capacity and Heat of Neutralization

**Subject:** General Chemistry Laboratory (CM011L)  
**Type:** Group Laboratory Experiment and Technical Report  
**Date:** September 2026

### Project Overview

This experiment explored the principles of heat transfer, energy conservation, and chemical reactions through calorimetry.

My group and I investigated how thermal energy moves between substances at different temperatures and how heat is released during an acid-base neutralization reaction.

The activity involved measuring temperature changes, calculating heat transfer, and determining the heat of neutralization using mathematical formulas.

### Objectives

The objectives were to determine the heat capacity of a calorimeter, examine the transfer of thermal energy, and calculate the heat released during the neutralization of hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide.

### Results and Findings

In the calorimeter calibration, the first trial produced a heat capacity of 29.89 J/°C. The second trial produced an inconsistent negative calculated value, highlighting possible measurement uncertainty.

During the neutralization experiment, both trials showed an increase in temperature, indicating that the reaction released heat.

The calculated average heat of neutralization was **−60.49 kJ/mol**, demonstrating the exothermic nature of the reaction.

### What I Learned

This experiment helped me appreciate how mathematics can be used to explain and measure physical processes.

I learned that even small differences in temperature measurements can significantly affect calculated results. The activity also emphasized the importance of evaluating unexpected findings rather than simply accepting numerical answers.

These lessons are relevant to AI Engineering because computational models and analytical methods must be checked for accuracy, consistency, and reasonable results.
